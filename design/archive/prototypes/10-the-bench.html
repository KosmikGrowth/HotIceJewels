A commission takes six weeks. Here is every week of it.

Most workshops keep this part hidden, which is why commissioning anything feels like handing over money and waiting. Below is the whole sequence — what happens, what it costs at that point, and exactly how much of it you can still change your mind about.

1.0

The conversation

An hour at the counter or on a call. You bring a reference — a photo, a screenshot, a piece you already own, or nothing at all — and we work out what you're actually after, which is often not the thing you walked in describing.

We'll talk budget here rather than at the end. It's the single most useful constraint in the room, and knowing it early means we design something you can pay for instead of something you have to talk yourself out of.

  • DurationAbout an hour
  • You payNothing
  • Still changeableEverything

Leaves with: a written brief and a price range, both on paper.

2.0

The drawing

One of the bench draws it by hand, then builds it in CAD so you can turn it around on screen. You get both — the graphite because it shows intent, the render because it shows proportion.

Revisions at this stage are free and unlimited, and most commissions take two or three. This is the cheapest possible moment to change your mind, so we'd rather you used it hard.

  • Duration3–5 days
  • You payNothing
  • RevisionsUnlimited

Leaves with: a signed drawing and a fixed quote that won't move.

3.0

The wax and the pour

The model is printed in wax, checked against the drawing, and invested for casting. Once the metal goes in, the shape is committed — this is the point of no return, and it's why the deposit sits here and not earlier.

You're welcome to come and watch the pour. Most people don't, and the ones who do usually say it's the part they remember.

  • DurationAbout a week
  • You payHalf, as deposit
  • Still changeableFinish and stones

Leaves with: nothing yet — but the shape is now fixed.

4.0

The setting

The longest stage and the one that decides whether the piece reads as good. Stones are seated by hand under a scope, one at a time, and a densely set pendant can hold a setter for a fortnight without looking like much is happening.

You'll get photographs partway through. They will look worse than the finished piece — setting looks rough right up until the final polish, and we'd rather show you that than a staged shot.

  • Duration1–3 weeks
  • You payNothing further
  • Still changeablePolish only

Leaves with: progress photographs, unretouched.

5.0

The hand-over

We weigh it in front of you, put it on the scale next to the quote, and hand you the original drawing framed. The balance is due here — not before — and if the piece isn't what the drawing promised, you don't pay it.

After that it's yours, and the repair terms start: free resizing, re-tipping, re-polishing and stone resetting for as long as the bench exists.

  • DurationHalf an hour
  • You payThe balance
  • AftercareNo time limit

Leaves with: the piece, the drawing, and the scale reading.
