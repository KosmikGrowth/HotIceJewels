What you get told. What's actually true.

Five things you will hear in almost any jeweller's, side by side with what is going on underneath them. None of this is secret. It is just not usually said out loud while somebody is holding your card.

You get told

"This piece is 60% off today."

The tag shows a price crossed out and a lower one beside it, and the lower one still feels like a lot, which is how you know it is a good deal.

What's true

The crossed-out number was never charged.

A permanent discount is not a discount — it is the price, with a decoy printed above it. The trade calls this anchoring and it works on nearly everyone, including people who know exactly what it is.

Here: one price, set off the gram weight, and no sales. If we could sell it for less we would have priced it less.

No sales · no discount codes · no launch pricing

You get told

"It's 18-karat gold."

Said about the finish, the colour, or the plating — and technically accurate in all three cases, which is what makes it useful.

What's true

Karat describes purity, not depth.

An 18-karat plating is a few microns of 18-karat gold over brass. The sentence is true and the object is not what you think you are buying.

Here: the only correct follow-up question is "solid or plated", and the only answer we ever give is solid, because that is all we hold.

Ask any shop that question and watch the pause

You get told

"Certified conflict-free."

Usually accompanied by a certificate in a plastic sleeve with a serial number on it and a logo you have not seen before.

What's true

It certifies a paper trail, not a mine.

The scheme it refers to covers rough stones crossing borders. It is not nothing, and it is also not a statement about the conditions the stone was dug in.

Here: lab-grown by default, because the provenance question has an actual answer — a reactor, in a building, with an address.

Mined stock is labelled and the supplier is named

You get told

"It's an investment piece."

Offered gently, near the end, usually while the piece is already around your neck and you are looking in the mirror.

What's true

Retail sells above melt and buys below it.

That spread is real, it is wide, and it does not close quickly. Jewellery can hold value. It is not a way of making money, and anyone framing it that way is selling.

Here: buy it because you want to wear it. We will not put a resale figure on a piece, including our own.

We do not quote appreciation · ever

You get told

"Lifetime warranty included."

Printed on the back of the receipt, in a paragraph nobody reads at the counter because the piece is right there in front of them.

What's true

Read the exclusions before you need them.

Most exclude normal wear, which is the only thing that ever actually happens to jewellery. Worn prongs and stretched links are what a warranty is for, and they are usually the first two things carved out of it.

Here: repairs are free with no registration, no receipt, no window, and no list of exclusions — including on pieces you did not buy from us.

Nothing to register · nothing excluded
