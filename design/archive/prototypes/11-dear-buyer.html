ICE JEWELS Write back

Written at the bench · Ice Jewels

The fourth of March

Dear buyer,

You have almost certainly been lied to about jewellery, and I would like to start by apologising on behalf of a trade that has made a business model out of it. Not dramatically — nobody sold you a brick. But the number on the tag had very little to do with what was in the box, and somebody in the chain knew that and said nothing.

I spent eleven years on the other side of that counter. I was good at it. I could take a chain that cost four hundred pounds in metal and labour and talk someone into two thousand by describing the clarity of stones they would never look at under a loupe. Nobody was defrauded. Everything I said was technically true. It was still a con, and I knew it was a con, and I did it for eleven years.

What finally stopped me was a man buying a chain for his son.

He came in with cash in an envelope. He had saved for it. He asked me, plainly, whether it was a good piece, and I gave him the answer I always gave — I talked about the finish and the weight and the way the light moved on it, and I let him buy something at four times what it was worth because he did not know the questions to ask and I was not going to teach him.

He was delighted. That is the part I still think about. He left happy, and I had taken about fourteen hundred pounds off a man who was buying a present for his kid. I went home and I did not sleep, and about six weeks later I left.

The trade's whole advantage is that you don't know what to ask. So this shop is built on telling you first.

Which is why this place works the way it does.

Every piece has its gram weight on the tag, and the price divided by that weight, so you can see exactly what you are paying per gram and compare it to anyone. We publish what a plain chain costs us to make. We put the scale on the counter and weigh the thing in front of you before you pay, every time, without being asked.

We will also talk you down. If you come in asking for VVS stones I will tell you that you cannot see the difference at conversational distance and that VS will save you a third. If you want a heavier chain than you need, I will say so. I lose money on that conversation roughly once a week and it is still the best decision in the shop.

None of this makes us saints. We are a business, we take a margin, and it is written down where you can see it. The difference is only that you get to check the arithmetic — which should be the least a jeweller offers, and is somehow still unusual.

You do not have to trust me. That is rather the point.

Trust is what a shop asks for when it does not want to show you the numbers. I would rather hand you the numbers and let you decide whether they are good. Come in, bring something you already own, and I will test it and tell you what it is even if you never buy anything here. Quite a lot of people do exactly that, and a fair few of them come back.

Since
The bench opened in 2019 with one torch, one vice, and a secondhand scale that is still on the counter.
The scale
Calibrated quarterly. The certificate is on the wall behind it, because someone once asked and it was a fair question.
What we won't do
Sell plated goods, quote a resale value, or price a piece by what we think you can afford to pay for it.
Repairs
Free, no time limit, whether or not the piece came from us.

Yours, from the bench

The founder · Ice Jewels · Written by hand, typed by someone kinder to keyboards

P.S. — If you have read this far and you are only here to find out whether the chain you already own is real, the answer is that we will test it for free and it takes about two minutes. You do not need an appointment and you will not be sold anything.

Write back, or come and put something on the scale.
